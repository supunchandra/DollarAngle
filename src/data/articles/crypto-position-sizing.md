Crypto discussions often begin with the wrong question:

**“Do you think Bitcoin will go up?”**

A stronger question comes first:

**“If this entire investment thesis fails, what happens to my financial plan?”**

That is a position-sizing question.

The SEC's Investor.gov materials repeatedly warn that crypto assets can be exceptionally volatile and speculative and that retail investors can face risks involving custody, platform failure, fraud, regulation and loss of access.

None of that proves a specific crypto asset will fail. It means the risk deserves to be sized honestly.

## Separate the technology thesis from the portfolio decision

You can believe blockchain technology will remain important and still decide that a specific token is overpriced.

You can believe Bitcoin has a credible long-term use case and still decide that a 40% portfolio allocation is too much for your circumstances.

You can dislike crypto and still acknowledge that some investors use it as a speculative allocation.

The investment decision is not simply “bullish or bearish.” It is also about **price, custody, time horizon and portfolio impact**.

## Treat speculative capital differently

Money needed for rent, an emergency fund, next year's tuition or a near-term house purchase has a job that does not match crypto volatility.

Speculative allocations should generally come after basic financial resilience, not before it.

Investor.gov's educational materials state that investors should think about how much of their overall portfolio, if any, should be allocated to crypto and only put at risk money they can afford to lose entirely.

That framing is useful because it turns a vague risk warning into a practical constraint.

## Custody is part of the investment risk

With traditional securities, investors often focus on price and company fundamentals.

Crypto adds operational questions:

- Who controls the private keys?
- Is the asset held in self-custody or by a third party?
- What happens if the custodian fails?
- Is there insurance, and what exactly does it cover?
- Can withdrawals be suspended?
- How are seed phrases and recovery methods protected?

The SEC's 2025 custody bulletin explains that losing a private key in self-custody can permanently remove access, while third-party custody introduces counterparty and platform risks.

## “Proof of reserves” is not the same as a financial-statement audit

Crypto platforms sometimes publish reserve attestations or similar reports.

Investor.gov has warned that these should not automatically be treated as equivalent to audits conducted under the standards that apply to public-company financial statements.

For an investor, this means platform claims deserve scrutiny rather than blind trust.

## Position size changes emotional behavior

A position that is financially survivable is easier to hold rationally.

If a 30% decline causes panic because you need the money soon, the position may have been too large or the time horizon too short.

Crypto markets can move far more than that. Position sizing should reflect that reality before the volatility arrives.

## Diversification still matters

Owning ten different tokens does not necessarily create meaningful diversification if they all depend on the same market liquidity and risk appetite.

Correlations can rise sharply during stress.

If crypto is part of a broader portfolio, evaluate its risk relative to stocks, bonds, cash, real estate and your human capital—not just relative to other crypto assets.

## Beware yield that hides counterparty risk

A platform offering unusually high yield is not creating return from nowhere.

Ask:

- Who is borrowing the assets?
- What collateral exists?
- Can the platform rehypothecate deposits?
- What legal claim do customers have in bankruptcy?
- Is the advertised yield variable?

High yield can be compensation for high risk.

## The DollarAngle

You do not need certainty about crypto's future to make a sensible portfolio decision.

Decide how much permanent loss you can tolerate, understand custody, and keep speculative assets from becoming essential-money assets.

Confidence is not risk management. Position size is.
